# Fraud & abusive-betting detection: a sourced brief

**Prepared:** 2026-11-04 · **For:** SportsDesk security/fraud plugin (GSLive manager logs + F402 tickets) · **Status:** prior-art research, not a model validation.

**Evidence rule.** Every claim is linked. "Observed" = a captured log row or a verified ruling; "derived" = computed by a rule/score; "fixture" = synthetic/test data. A missing source is reported as missing, never as zero. No personal data or credentials are reproduced here.

**Source mix.** Regulators/standards (UKGC, MGA, NIST, SR 11-7), supplier technical documentation (Sportradar), vendor research (SEON, GeoComply, Sumsub) and peer-reviewed/preprint research (Saito; Heirene; arXiv). Vendor figures are vendor claims; preprints are not peer-reviewed.

## 1. Courtsiding / rink-side latency arbitrage

- **What it is.** Courtsiding is the "instant, covert transmission of information about developments in a sporting event" that lets gamblers beat wagering providers; the related practice of data-scouting captures live scores/statistics for betting operators, officially or unofficially. The mechanism is explicitly "exploits streaming/broadcast delays" ([Sport Integrity Australia](https://web.archive.org/web/20250315180931/https://www.sportintegrity.gov.au/sites/default/files/SIA115-0123-Courtsiding%20and%20Data%20Scouting-E-1%20%5BDIGITAL%5D.pdf)).
- **Why it is structurally possible.** In-play broadcasts are delayed, so UKGC rules require operators to tell customers that "live" TV is delayed and that others may have more up-to-date information ([UKGC RTS 15](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-15-in-play-betting)). The operator cannot eliminate the latency gap; it can only price/delay around it.
- **Supplier-side detection.** Sportradar's Late Bet Score is a machine-learning account score (0.00–1.00) for "late-bet abuse during live betting", built on "timing anomalies, sudden value spikes, and patterns that suggest market-aware latency abuse"; ≥0.90 is "strong likelihood" and is used to set a dynamic **Live Time Delay**, a delay offset, or manual review ([Sportradar LBS FAQ](https://docs.sportradar.com/itservices/models/late-bet-score-lbs-api/faqs.md)). This is the clearest public description of the production pattern: per-account behavioural score → live acceptance delay → manual escalation.
- **Feed-side telemetry.** Sportradar's Latency Indicator assigns each match an expected latency level from historical reference data (Low ~0–2 s, Moderate ~4 s, High ~8 s, Very High ~12 s, Exceptional 16+ s), updates it live when delay crosses a threshold, and lets "operational teams override the classification" ([Sportradar Latency Indicator](https://docs.sportradar.com/live-data/latency-indicator-beta.md)). That is the feed-side signal needed to separate a fast bettor from a stalled feed.
- **In-venue data manipulation is a distinct threat.** An ITIA ruling found two ITF chair umpires manipulated entries on a handheld electronic scoring device "to facilitate the betting activities of third parties"; both were found liable under the Tennis Anti-Corruption Program ([ITIA ruling](https://itia.tennis/media/u1bjcbve/decision-itia-v-khudoiani-movsisyan-am-_redacted.pdf)). That is official-data manipulation, not courtsiding, but it produces the same bet-to-score timing signature.
- **Detection pattern (design synthesis).** The published components are: bet timing relative to scoring events, acceptance delay vs. configured delay, account-level late-bet scoring, and feed-latency context. A detector should therefore compute `t_accept − t_score` per bet, normalise by the match's latency level, and treat a cluster of bets accepted just before a score change as the unit of evidence — not a single bet ([LBS](https://docs.sportradar.com/itservices/models/late-bet-score-lbs-api/faqs.md), [Latency Indicator](https://docs.sportradar.com/live-data/latency-indicator-beta.md)).

## 2. Bonus abuse and multi-accounting (sybil) detection

- **Regulatory baseline.** UK licence condition 17 requires remote operators to obtain and verify a customer's name, address and date of birth before that customer gambles; accepting initials, nicknames, commercial addresses, or fuzzy/partial matches is not sufficient, and identity concerns must not be deferred to withdrawal ([UKGC blog, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/blog/post/reminder-of-identity-verification-requirements-for-remote-operators)).
- **Multiple accounts are a licensed possibility, not a free-for-all.** Malta's Player Protection Directive contemplates operators allowing more than one account per brand (or across brands without separate registration), but limits and self-exclusion must then apply across all accounts; it also requires "analytical tools and/or behaviour monitoring systems with pre-designed and/or evolving parameters", documented criteria that include the "use of multiple payment methods" and the amount/frequency of deposits and wagers, and retained records of investigations ([MGA directive](https://www.mga.org.mt/app/uploads/Directive-2-of-2018-Player-Protection-Directive.pdf)).
- **Tactics.** Promo abusers "reset devices to look new", spoof location to appear eligible, use synthetic or stolen IDs, and script the same play across hundreds of accounts; device farms cluster many users and devices at one physical location. GeoComply's pitch is to read "location, device, and behaviour together" and to surface "network graphs" rather than one account at a time ([GeoComply](https://www.geocomply.com/promo-abuse/)).
- **Four signup signal layers.** A vendor detection stack runs device fingerprinting, email/phone enrichment, IP intelligence and behavioural signals in parallel; reused device IDs, shared phone numbers/identity fragments, and form-fill velocity/copy-paste/keystroke dynamics are the discriminators, with data-centre/VPN/residential-proxy IPs as supporting evidence ([SEON](https://seon.io/resources/igaming-registration-fraud-detection/)).
- **Account-burst detection.** A fraud ring can run hundreds of accounts off one device farm; the cheap control point is registration, before KYC spend and before a bonus is seeded ([SEON](https://seon.io/resources/igaming-registration-fraud-detection/)). Operationally this is a velocity predicate: new accounts per device/IP-subnet/payment instrument per hour.
- **False-positive discipline.** Shared devices and VPNs have innocent explanations (household, privacy), so no single flag is conclusive; the signal is the cluster of weak flags across a group ([SEON](https://seon.io/resources/igaming-registration-fraud-detection/)).
- **KYC staging.** Cheap checks belong at registration, heavier document/biometric KYC at first deposit and first withdrawal, and a login-time comparison against the known device/location/cadence baseline for account takeover ([SEON](https://seon.io/resources/igaming-registration-fraud-detection/)).

## 3. Match-fixing and suspicious-bet monitoring

- **Industry monitoring is a distinct function with its own platforms.** IBIA's public monitoring surface is a "Global Monitoring & Alert Platform" ([IBIA](https://www.ibia.bet/monitoring)). Sportradar's 2024 integrity report counted **1,108 suspicious matches across 12 sports in 95 countries**, says AI became the primary monitoring tool for handball, table tennis, tennis and volleyball, and reports its Integrity Exchange of **117 betting operators** generated 291 suspicious-match identifications from operator-provided data ([Sportradar Integrity in Action 2024](http://web.archive.org/web/20250209132910/https://storage.pardot.com/533382/1736355595fvYFfMLa/Sportradar_Integrity_in_Action_2024.pdf)).
- **Esports taxonomy in practice.** ESIC's 2016 presentation to the Nevada Gaming Policy Committee names its sources as "Sportradar FDS & gambling industry alerts", spanning 450 bookmakers (80+ offering esports), alongside participant education, an intelligence database, and "alerts to participants" ([ESIC presentation](https://gaming.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/gamingnvgov/content/about/committees/ESIC-Integrity-in-esports-Presentation.pdf)).
- **In-play is the hard case.** In-play is roughly 40% of betting volume, and verified match-fixing ground truth is scarce; the published approach models expected in-play betting volume from match state (a state-space model) and flags deviations with outlier detection, because unusual odds movements or substantially elevated stakes are the observable traces ([Winkelmann et al., arXiv 2605.30209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30209)).
- **Alert ingredients.** Across those sources the recurring inputs are: abnormal odds movement/velocity, stake/volume anomalies relative to a match-state baseline, cross-operator comparison, and linkage to accounts/agents that move together. Cross-operator data is why the Integrity Exchange matters ([Sportradar 2024](http://web.archive.org/web/20250209132910/https://storage.pardot.com/533382/1736355595fvYFfMLa/Sportradar_Integrity_in_Action_2024.pdf)); group-level structure is the graph case ([graph survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11299)).
- **"Steam move" is desk jargon, not a regulatory term.** The computable version is a sudden cross-book repricing; the literature's measurable proxies are unusual odds movement and elevated stake/volume versus baseline ([arXiv 2605.30209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30209)). Name the feature `STEAM_*`, but document that the threshold is desk-calibrated, not regulator-defined.
- **Liability and line consistency.** Trading systems enforce per-ticket liability limits and can reject a ticket with `-703 Liability limit exceeded` ([Transaction 3.0](https://docs.sportradar.com/transaction30api/sandbox/user-manual/rejected_liability_limit.md)); MTS is explicitly a "risk and liability management service" ([Sportradar MTS](https://docs.sportradar.com/mts/managed-trading-services.md)). Cross-book line inconsistency is the comparison case, which a 117-operator exchange is built to surface ([Sportradar 2024](http://web.archive.org/web/20250209132910/https://storage.pardot.com/533382/1736355595fvYFfMLa/Sportradar_Integrity_in_Action_2024.pdf)).

## 4. Chip dumping, agent-donor schemes, and "donor shield"

- **Chip dumping defined.** One player deliberately loses chips to an accomplice ("the receiver") to transfer value; the mechanics look like ordinary bad play (calling a raise that should be folded, betting into a hand that cannot win, folding a strong hand to a small bet), which is why intent is hard to prove. Adjacent collusion includes soft play and "whipsawing" a third player; operators tailor response, and chip dumping tied to unexplained funding should trigger a financial-crime review ([Sumsub](https://sumsub.com/blog/chip-dumping-guide/)).
- **Regulator expectation.** UKGC RTS 11 (peer-to-peer) requires measures to "deter, prevent, and detect collusion and cheating", retention of relevant activity records, and investigation records; its detection examples include players who frequently share tables, players from the same address sharing a table, "suspicious patterns of play (such as chip dumping)", and unusual gameplay statistics ([UKGC RTS 11](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-11-limiting-collusion-and-cheating)).
- **Agent-based bookmaking analog (inference, not a published taxonomy).** In an agent/credit book the same value transfer can be expressed as a linked player winning against an agent's book and settling outside it; the chip-dumping literature supplies the intent test (deliberate loss, one-directional value, linked identities) and RTS 11 supplies the evidence standard ([Sumsub](https://sumsub.com/blog/chip-dumping-guide/), [RTS 11](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-11-limiting-collusion-and-cheating)).
- **"Donor shield" is desk jargon.** This repo's governor qualifies a donor at ≥ $50,000 lifetime house net and treats donor containment as a distinct enforcement tier; "Trojan asymmetry" is the pattern of small base losses followed by step-up wins (≥ $1,000 net, ≥ 15% ROI) ([`lib/gslive_surveillance_governor.ts`](../../lib/gslive_surveillance_governor.ts)). Conventionally, the underlying behaviours are chip dumping/soft play, and a high-value legitimate-looking account used as cover is a masking/layering structure — there is no standard industry term "donor shield".
- **What to detect.** One-directional net flow between linked accounts/agents, repeated same-table or same-event pairing, settlement shortly after placement, stake/odds behaviour inconsistent with the account's history, and shared IP/device/payment links ([RTS 11](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-11-limiting-collusion-and-cheating), [Sumsub](https://sumsub.com/blog/chip-dumping-guide/)).

## 5. Model design practice

- **Production is hybrid, not "rules vs. ML".** Malta requires analytical/behaviour-monitoring tools with "pre-designed and/or evolving parameters" ([MGA](https://www.mga.org.mt/app/uploads/Directive-2-of-2018-Player-Protection-Directive.pdf)); Sportradar ships an ML account score with explicit thresholds ([LBS](https://docs.sportradar.com/itservices/models/late-bet-score-lbs-api/faqs.md)); its integrity monitoring uses AI as the primary tool in four sports ([Sportradar 2024](http://web.archive.org/web/20250209132910/https://storage.pardot.com/533382/1736355595fvYFfMLa/Sportradar_Integrity_in_Action_2024.pdf)); academic in-play detection fits a statistical baseline then applies outlier rules ([arXiv 2605.30209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30209)). The practical stack is deterministic predicates for known patterns, a statistical baseline for context, and an ML ranker for triage.
- **Weak labels are the norm.** Snorkel's framing is directly applicable: subject-matter experts write labelling functions "which can have unknown accuracies and correlations", and the system denoises their outputs "without access to ground truth" ([Snorkel, arXiv 1711.10160](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10160)). A model trained on rule-consensus labels is therefore a model of the rules, not of adjudicated fraud.
- **Imbalanced-data metrics.** On strongly imbalanced data, ROC plots can be "deceptive" about reliability; precision–recall plots are more informative because they evaluate the fraction of true positives among positive predictions ([Saito & Rehmsmeier, PLOS ONE 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432)). Operationally, report PR-AUC, precision@k for the analyst queue, calibration, and label support — not accuracy or AUC alone.
- **Explainability is a design requirement.** NIST's AI RMF treats explainability/interpretability as trustworthiness characteristics: explainable systems "can be debugged and monitored more easily" and lend themselves to "more thorough documentation, audit, and governance"; maintaining provenance of training data supports transparency and accountability ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)).
- **Governance and audit.** SR 11-7's validation framework has three elements — conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring (process verification and benchmarking), and outcomes analysis/back-testing — and requires documentation detailed enough that parties unfamiliar with the model can understand how it operates, its limitations and key assumptions ([SR 11-7](https://web.archive.org/web/2025id_/https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107a1.pdf)). Thresholds and feature versions are therefore governed artifacts, not config tweaks.
- **Threshold review.** Because the score cutoffs drive live delay and manual review ([LBS](https://docs.sportradar.com/itservices/models/late-bet-score-lbs-api/faqs.md)), each threshold needs an owner, a review date, and a measured basis; MGA's "pre-designed and/or evolving parameters" language anticipates exactly that ([MGA](https://www.mga.org.mt/app/uploads/Directive-2-of-2018-Player-Protection-Directive.pdf)).

## 6. Feature vocabulary actually used

- **Per bet:** time-to-event and accept delay, odds placed vs. accepted (drift), implied probability, stake and to-win, line, period, state code, hour/day, and result ([LBS](https://docs.sportradar.com/itservices/models/late-bet-score-lbs-api/faqs.md) describes timing and value-spike inputs; the concrete GSLive fields are `logTime`, `delay`, `accept`, `fVal`/`fVal_new`, `bet`, `toWin`, `line`, `period`, `state`, `result`).
- **Per account:** prior bet count, prior stake mean, prior drift/reversal/bot rates, prior IP count ([contract](../security/SECURITY-PLUGIN-CONTRACT.md)), and behavioural aggregates — age, deposits per active day, average stake, days since betting were the most predictive account variables in a 1,470-customer sports/race betting study, with 30 days of data enough for AUROC 0.74–0.75 ([Heirene et al. 2026](https://real.mtak.hu/243558/)).
- **Sharpness / CLV:** closing odds are the standard reference point for measuring whether a bet beat the market; a strategy using publicly available odds was profitable over a 10-year closing-odds simulation, and the authors describe the industry's "discriminatory practices against successful clients" ([Beating the bookies, arXiv 1710.02824](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)). CLV vs. the closing line is therefore a sharp-account feature, and account limiting is a confounder for later behaviour.
- **Network/graph:** fraud can appear as a distinctive edge, node, or larger subgraph — e.g., a group of clients engaged in money-laundering schemes ([graph survey, arXiv 1910.11299](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11299)). For this desk: shared IP, /24 (or /64) prefix, device ID where available, payment instrument, agent, and same-event co-occurrence.
- **IP/device novelty:** reused device IDs, shared phone/identity fragments, data-centre/VPN/residential-proxy classes, and many accounts per device/location ([SEON](https://seon.io/resources/igaming-registration-fraud-detection/), [GeoComply](https://www.geocomply.com/promo-abuse/)).
- **Derived, not yet validated here:** win-rate deviation vs. the closing-line expectation, stake volatility (coefficient of variation), session counts, market/selection entropy, bet-type mix, and "time since last score" are reasonable candidates, but they are design proposals; label them `derived` until measured against outcomes ([NIST provenance](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)).

## 7. Honest limits

- **Rule-consensus labels are not fraud labels.** A proxy label built from `isSuspectedBot`, `reversedCredit`, or `manualLogExists` encodes prior desk decisions and their errors; weak-supervision labels have unknown accuracies and correlations by construction ([Snorkel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10160)). Say "proxy label", never "probability of fraud".
- **Base rates and precision.** With rare positives, a high AUC can coexist with unusable precision; PR curves and precision@k are the honest reporting surface ([Saito & Rehmsmeier](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432)).
- **Evidence tiers must travel with every finding.** Distinguish observed (captured row, verified ruling), derived (rule/score output), and fixture (synthetic/test) data; NIST ties training-data provenance to transparency and accountability ([NIST](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)), and SR 11-7 requires documented limitations ([SR 11-7](https://web.archive.org/web/2025id_/https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107a1.pdf)).
- **Regulators expect an auditable explanation.** SR 11-7's documentation standard (an unfamiliar party can understand operation, limitations, assumptions) and NIST's audit/governance rationale set the bar ([SR 11-7](https://web.archive.org/web/2025id_/https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107a1.pdf), [NIST](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)); MGA requires records of investigations and their outcomes ([MGA](https://www.mga.org.mt/app/uploads/Directive-2-of-2018-Player-Protection-Directive.pdf)); UKGC RTS 11 requires investigation records including reason, outcome and evidence ([RTS 11](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-11-limiting-collusion-and-cheating)).
- **A model validated only against its own rules is a consistency check.** It can show the pipeline works; it cannot show the detector finds real fraud. State that in the model card and in the UI.

**Prior-art naming.** `courtsiding` / `courtside data-scouting`; `late-bet abuse` / `latency arbitrage` / `live time delay (LTD)`; `multi-accounting` / `sybil accounts` / `promo (bonus) abuse` / `device farm` / `account takeover (ATO)`; `match-fixing` / `spot-fixing` / `suspicious betting alert` / `steam move` / `line movement`; `chip dumping` / `soft play` / `collusion` / `whipsawing` / `value transfer` / `money mule`; `weak supervision` / `proxy label` / `PR-AUC` / `precision@k` / `model risk management` / `explainability`. "Donor" and "donor shield" are this desk's jargon, not standard industry terms.

## Design implications for the plugin (concrete, implementable)

1. **`COURTSIDE_WINDOW`** — per bet, compute `t_accept − t_last_score` and `t_place − t_last_score` for the same `eID`/`period`; flag accounts whose bets cluster in the final seconds before score changes. Fields: `userID`, `eID`, `period`, `logTime`, `accept`, plus score-event timestamps from `getEventLog`/scoreboard. Normalise by the match latency level.
2. **`FEED_STALL_DIVERGENCE`** — join feed-latency level/change events to bet arrivals; flag a burst of accepted bets while the feed is stalled or just after a stall. Fields: `expectedlatencylevelid`/latency-change events, `logTime`, `accept`, `delay`.
3. **`DELAY_ACCEPT_MISMATCH`** — compare `accept − delay` with the account's effective live delay and the house default; flag sub-delay acceptances and repeated near-zero accepts. Fields: `delay`, `accept`, `state`, `agent`.
4. **`LATE_BET_SCORE`** — a transparent daily per-account score (0–100) over late-bet rate, median `t_accept−t_score`, odds-drift-at-accept, and stake spike; publish the feature contributions and the cutoffs. Fields: `logTime`, `accept`, `delay`, `fVal`/`fVal_new`, `bet`, per-account history.
5. **`OVERRIDE_AUDIT`** — flag manual overrides and their effect: `manualLogExists`, `adminUser`, plus post-override accept latency and settlement time. Fields: `manualLogExists`, `adminUser`, `logTime`, `accept`, `state`.
6. **`IP_SUBNET_GRAPH`** — build account nodes from `ipAddress`, `/24` and `/64` prefixes, plus device/payment IDs where present; return masked clusters (never raw IP), with account count, agent count and cross-agent links. Fields: `ipAddress`, `customerID`/`userID`, `agent`, first/last seen.
7. **`ACCOUNT_BURST`** — new accounts and first bets per device/IP-subnet/payment instrument per hour; flag `> k` with a minimum cluster size of 3 and at least two independent signals. Fields: registration/first-bet timestamps, `ipAddress`, `customerID`, `agent`, bonus flag.
8. **`KYC_STAGE_GATE`** — flag bonus claims or withdrawals before full verification, name/DOB mismatch, and a payment instrument shared across accounts; report `insufficientData` when KYC/payment fields are absent. Fields: KYC state, bonus/withdrawal events, payment token, `customerID`.
9. **`AGENT_DONOR_FLOW`** — per agent↔account pair, compute one-directional net flow (player win vs. loss), stake concentration, and same-event pairing; flag pairs with extreme flow plus a shared IP/device link. Fields: `agent`, `userID`, `bet`, `toWin`, `result`, `ipAddress`; F402 `AgentID`, `CustomerID`, `AmountWagered`, `ToWinAmount`.
10. **`DONOR_SHIELD`** — qualify a donor at ≥ $50,000 lifetime house net, then require ≥ 2 independent links (IP/prefix + device/payment/agent) to a flagged cluster before escalation; surface the donor's value as a reason for review, not as exculpation. Fields: lifetime net, graph links, rule hits.
11. **`TROJAN_ASYMMETRY`** — detect the base-loss → step-up-win sequence (local constants: base net ≤ −$0.01, step-up net ≥ $1,000, step-up ROI ≥ 15%) in chronological account history. Fields: ordered bets, `bet`, `toWin`, `result`, account age.
12. **`CLV_DEVIATION`** — per account, mean CLV against the closing line, weighted by stake; flag persistent positive CLV + high stake (sharp) separately from late-bet abuse. Fields: accepted odds (`fVal`), closing odds snapshot, `bet`, `userID`.
13. **`MARKET_ENTROPY`** — Shannon entropy over `sport`/`period`/`wagerType`/`line` per account; low entropy + high volume is a specialisation signal. Mark `derived`; do not present it as validated fraud evidence.
14. **`SETTLE_BYPASS`** — flag bets settled unusually fast (< 30 s) with ≥ 2 corroborating signals (manual log, override, late placement). Fields: `state`, settlement timestamp, `manualLogExists`, `adminUser`.
15. **`EVIDENCE_ENVELOPE`** — every finding carries source id, path, sha256, age, provenance (`live-api`/`derived`/`fixture`/`missing`), masked IDs, and the rule version; refuse to fit or score on `fixture` sources. Fields: source registry, `reports/security/fraud-model.json` metadata.

**One-line build order:** start with the graph and provenance envelope (items 6, 15), then the timing detectors (1–5), then the value-transfer detectors (9–12); fit the weak-label ranker only after the proxy-label support and holdout windows are measured.
